Good ethics starts with better questions.

Your choices are a starting point, not a compliance verdict. Before a feature ships, a team still needs to map data flows, check its legal obligations, consult affected people, and test its safeguards.

Carry this into your next design reviewWhat is the minimum data this feature needs? Who can access it? How long does it stick around? Can a child or family say no without losing the core experience?
Lens 01 / harm

Who carries the risk?

A feature can make an app more engaging and still expose children to tracking, profiling, or a breach. Consider the people with the least power to opt out.

Lens 02 / agency

Is consent meaningful?

Clear notice and real choices matter. For children, ask whether the experience is understandable and whether refusing changes access to something essential.

Lens 03 / restraint

Could less be enough?

Data minimization is a design move: collect only what serves a stated purpose, limit access, and decide when it will be deleted.

02 / The legal floor

What does the law actually ask?

Ethics gives a team questions to reason through. Law sets obligations that depend on the facts. Here’s one U.S. federal example relevant to the fictional app.

United States / FederalChildren’s privacy

COPPA & the FTC Rule

The Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and the Federal Trade Commission’s implementing Rule can apply to commercial sites and services directed to children under 13 that collect personal information, and to general-audience services with actual knowledge they’re collecting it from children under 13. Covered operators have notice, verifiable parental consent, access/deletion, and security duties. The amended Rule adds separate parental consent for certain disclosures to third parties, including targeted advertising.

Who / whereU.S. federal law; coverage can reach foreign services directed to children in the U.S.
What countsPersonal information includes some persistent identifiers; passive tracking can count as collection.
Design promptMap every data flow—including SDKs and vendors—before the feature ships.
Not a shortcutA terms-of-service age limit alone does not settle whether a service is child-directed.

03 / Your evidence

Make the case yourself.

Write in your own words. Explain the ethics issue, connect it to the interaction you tried, then propose a specific revision. Finish by asking a peer to test the site and recording one improvement you made from their feedback.

1 / Explain the issue

What is the ethical concern in Little Atlas collecting progress data? Who could benefit, and who could be harmed? How is an ethical judgment different from COPPA’s legal duties?

2 / Explain one interaction

Name one choice you selected. What feedback did the site show, and how did it confirm or change your thinking?

3 / Propose a revision

Describe one concrete change you would make to the app or this site. Explain what problem it addresses and why.

4 / Record a peer test

Ask someone else to use the site. Avoid recording private details; initials or “classmate” are enough.

Quick peer-test route

Ask your tester to use a phone or computer and think aloud. Have them:

  1. Find the dilemma and explain what they are being asked to decide.
  2. Choose an option, read its feedback, continue through all three decisions, then restart.
  3. Tell you one part that was confusing or difficult. Make and record one improvement before submitting.
